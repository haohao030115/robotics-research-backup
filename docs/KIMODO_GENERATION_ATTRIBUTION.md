# Kimodo generation attribution and adaptation audit

This audit follows the accepted center episode `kimodo_fresh_20261016_03`, its source generation `kimodo_fresh_20261016_02`, the two earlier attempts, and a fixed-parameter five-seed center study. It answers what can be established from the recorded arrays and native qualification traces. It does not treat one successful center episode as evidence of spatial generalization.

## Accepted lineage

`kimodo_fresh_20261016_01` generated seed 0 successfully, then stopped at export with `ValueError: Calibration/task mismatch`: the frozen development calibration is identified as `kimodo-center-seed1-16p2-v1`. No downstream artifact was accepted.

`kimodo_fresh_20261016_02` generated seed 1 with the actual `Kimodo-G1-RP-v1` checkpoint, exported a complete 810-frame reference, and completed native SONIC/MuJoCo replay. It achieved 0.1236559 m maximum lift and 4.630 s hold, but `expert_valid=false` because `right_hand_thumb_2_link` made table support contact for 8 physics steps.

`kimodo_fresh_20261016_03` reused the exact seed-1 raw generation and changed only the export clearance from 0.03 m to 0.05 m. Contact refinement, approach refinement, calibration, SONIC, Hand PD, MuJoCo scene, and acceptance gates were unchanged. Native replay then produced `physical_success=true`, `expert_valid=true`, 0.1202921 m maximum lift, 4.575 s hold, and zero support-contact steps.

The accepted source hashes, checkpoint hashes, manifests, and failure records are in `/home/group2/outputs/canonical_production/production_summary.json`.

## Actual transformation chain

The source generation is `research/kimodo/generation/generate_grasp_motion.py`. `prepare_grasp_task.py` creates the fixed-center task, 487 source frames at 30 Hz, pose constraints, initial state, and hand schedule. The model emits body/root motion fields (`local_rot_mats`, `root_positions`, `posed_joints`, global rotations, root fields, and contacts); it does not emit the final 14-DOF dexterous hand trajectory used by SONIC. The hand trajectory comes from `task_plan.npz` and is then modified by the frozen hand preclose schedule.

The generation process seeds Python-visible model randomness with `torch.manual_seed(seed)`, `torch.musa.manual_seed_all(seed)`, and `numpy.random.seed(seed)` immediately before inference. The model is called five times for the five segmented prompts, with 120/120/60/60/127 frames and 100 denoising steps. The checkpoint and local text encoder are fixed by environment and recorded in `generation.json`. The accepted run is a true model generation (`model_generation_run=true`), not a fixture or rule motion.

`research/kimodo/export/export_kimodo_reference.py` performs these stages:

| Stage | Source/function | Accepted-run behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Raw conversion | `resample()` | 30 Hz to 50 Hz, linear interpolation for translational/joint columns, quaternion SLERP for root quaternion; 487 to 810 frames. |
| Joint mapping | `canonical()` and final array construction | Maps Kimodo source order to the canonical SONIC 29-joint order; units remain radians/meters. |
| Hand schedule | `main()` hand-preclose block | Replaces the right-hand plan after 7 s with a fixed cubic preclose to 40% at 8 s and full closure at 10 s; derives hand velocity by `numpy.gradient`. |
| Wrist target construction | `adapt()` | Computes world-frame wrist FK from the converted body pose, then uses the task plan wrist trajectory as the contact target. |
| Contact refinement | `adapt()` with `contact_plan` | Enabled. Position correction is clipped to 0.005 m and rotation correction to 0.04 rad, blended from 1–2 s. |
| Tracking calibration | `adapt()` calibration branch | Enabled only in accepted seed 1. It subtracts fixed measured world-frame close/hold position and rotation biases, blended over 6–10 s. The calibration is manually measured development evidence and is not generated by Kimodo. |
| Clearance | `adapt()` clearance branch | Accepted run uses +0.05 m vertical clearance blended over 4–7 s, decaying over 6–8 s. Lift margin is 0.0 m. |
| IK | `GraspReference.plan.ik()` in `adapt()` | Re-solves the body arm against the target wrist pose for every 50 Hz frame. The accepted maximum IK position error is 9.98e-6 m. |
| SONIC export | `export_reference()` | Writes canonical `reference.npz`, 810-frame motion CSVs, metadata, source hashes, and adapted per-frame diagnostics. |

The accepted export metadata reports a maximum wrist position correction of 0.072438 m and maximum rotation correction of 0.256447 rad. Those are adapter changes measured against the generated wrist FK; they are large enough to affect grasp behavior materially. The final reference also has a nonzero raw frame-zero report before the canonical reset, which is resolved by the canonical frame construction and validated output checks.

Native replay is implemented by `replay_validate_kimodo.py` and `run_sonic_grasp.py`. The original SONIC binary tracks the exported reference; physics runs at 200 Hz and references at 50 Hz. The recorded qualification contains `body_target_q`, `body_actual_q`, `hand_target_q`, `hand_actual_q`, contacts, forces, and every physics state. The accepted run had no missing policy ticks, no external force, no weld, no runtime body IK, and no fall. Hand PD is fixed at Kp=12 and Kd=0.3 in the recorded provenance.

## Quantitative stage comparison

The deterministic comparison arrays are in `/home/group2/outputs/kimodo_attribution_20261016_v2/stage_comparisons/seed_1.npz` through `seed_5.npz`; aggregate metrics are in `/home/group2/outputs/kimodo_attribution_20261016_v2/study_metrics.json`.

For the fixed center adapter study, raw 30 Hz motion was resampled to 50 Hz and compared in canonical joint order before any cross-stage statistic was computed. Across the five seeds, raw-to-final body joint RMS differences were approximately 0.498–0.502 rad. Raw wrist-to-final-target RMS position differences were 0.0222–0.0229 m, with maximum adapter position corrections of 0.0677–0.0742 m. The seed-1 accepted run has a 0.5004 rad body RMS difference, 0.0228 m wrist RMS difference, and 0.0724 m maximum correction.

The phase-resolved seed-1 body RMS differences are 0.414 rad (approach), 0.514 rad (contact), 0.490 rad (closure), 0.527 rad (lift), and 0.541 rad (hold). Wrist raw-to-target RMS errors are 0.0250, 0.0217, 0.0128, 0.0173, and 0.0252 m for those phases. The hand closure schedule begins at about 7.02 s. Executed SONIC body tracking versus target has 0.1123 rad RMS and 0.4535 rad maximum error in the seed-1 fixed-adapter replay.

Pairwise body RMS diversity across the five raw generations averages 0.05183 rad and reaches 0.06652 rad. After the fixed adapter, it averages 0.00443 rad and reaches 0.00599 rad. This is a roughly 8–12x reduction in measured body trajectory diversity. The study therefore shows strong convergence toward a common task-conditioned motion template, even though the raw Kimodo outputs differ.

## Fixed five-seed study

The predeclared seeds were 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5. Every seed used the same center task, checkpoint, prompts, 100 denoising steps, `clearance=0.05`, contact refinement, approach refinement, no tracking calibration, and no per-seed tuning. The complete manifest is `/home/group2/outputs/kimodo_attribution_20261016_v2/study_manifest.json`.

All five generations and exports succeeded. All five physical replays lifted the cube and held it for about 4.84–4.89 s, but all five failed the unchanged expert gate because of robot/table support contact: 14, 17, 20, 14, and 16 support-contact steps for seeds 1–5. Their qualification JSON files remain under each seed directory and are included in the denominator. This establishes that physical lift success and expert validity are separate outcomes.

The first launch attempt is also retained under `/home/group2/outputs/kimodo_attribution_20261016/`; it failed before task preparation because the runner omitted the validated Kimodo import path. It produced no motion and was not included in study statistics.

## Ablation

The seed-1 no-contact-refinement ablation is under `/home/group2/outputs/kimodo_attribution_20261016_v2/ablations/seed1_no_contact_refine/`. It kept the same raw generation, clearance, scene, controller, and gates while disabling contact refinement and calibration. Export succeeded, but native replay achieved only 0.00648 m lift, zero qualifying hold, 37 support-contact steps, and `physical_success=false`; it is not a valid episode. This supports contact refinement as a material contributor to the accepted grasp, while the study does not isolate calibration from clearance because the accepted seed-1 calibration is tied to its measured seed-1 task identity.

## Spatial generalization

No new fresh Kimodo spatial-position pilot was accepted in this audit. The new controlled experiment fixes the center object position, so it can measure seed diversity and adaptation collapse but cannot establish spatial responsiveness. Existing historical spatial artifacts are producer-specific and are not used as fresh Kimodo evidence. The current evidence supports automated production only for the calibrated center configuration. Viable bounds, position-conditioned clearance, and multi-position acceptance rates remain unmeasured for fresh Kimodo data.

A valid next spatial pilot requires predeclared positions and seeds, a task-conditioned adapter that derives the target from each case, fixed reusable parameters, and the same native expert gate. It must report generation, export/IK, physical, forensic, and dataset acceptance separately. Scaling to hundreds of episodes also requires removing the seed/task-specific calibration dependency, reducing table-support contacts, and demonstrating that raw and adapted spatial variance remain distinct across the workspace.

## Attribution conclusion

The original Kimodo model supplies the raw body motion and root motion. The final grasp behavior is substantially determined by downstream engineering: 30-to-50 Hz conversion, canonical joint mapping, task wrist targets, per-frame arm IK, hand preclose scheduling, measured tracking-bias compensation, contact refinement, and clearance. The accepted seed-1 result succeeded because the same fresh generated motion was lifted by 0.02 m of additional clearance, eliminating the eight support contacts seen in `_02`; it was not a new generation or a candidate-selection change.

The five-seed study shows genuine raw variation but strong post-adaptation collapse and zero expert-valid episodes under the fixed reusable adapter. The system therefore does not yet support a claim of spatially diverse, automatically generated Kimodo expert data. It supports a validated center grasp pipeline with substantial adapter control and a documented path to a spatial pilot.
